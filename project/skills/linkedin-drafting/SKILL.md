# LinkedIn Drafting

## Job to Be Done

Turn a researched Content Item into a natural, senior LinkedIn draft.

Use this skill in Editorial Studio after the thesis is sufficiently researched.

## Required Resources

Use:
- `voice-and-style.md`
- `content-strategy.md`
- `linkedin-profile.md`
- `best-posts.md` when populated
- `editorial-quality-bar.md`

## Inputs

Required:
- Content Item ID
- final/revised thesis
- evidence-backed facts
- senior BA implication
- practical insight

Helpful:
- series
- desired length
- visual concept
- relevant real work observation safe to share

## Workflow

1. Identify the one sentence the reader should remember.
2. Write a hook that introduces tension, change, or consequence without clickbait.
3. Explain the shift/problem.
4. Make the BA implication explicit.
5. Add a practical insight, decision lens, or operating-model consequence.
6. Close with a thought or question that advances the thesis.
7. Remove generic AI/LinkedIn phrasing.
8. Remove unsupported claims.
9. Tighten by 10–15% when possible.
10. Run the voice self-check.

## Preferred Shape

`Hook → shift/problem → BA implication → practical insight → strong closing thought/question`

Do not force visible section labels.

## Output Contract

Return:
- Content Item ID
- Draft
- Evidence-sensitive claims to verify, if any
- Optional note on what changed from the researched thesis

Status may become `Draft` only as a workflow state.

A finished draft is not approval.

## Quality Checks

- Does it sound authored rather than templated?
- Is the senior BA implication unmistakable?
- Is it specific enough to be defensible?
- Is the rhythm natural?
- Is there an em dash? If yes, remove it.
- Is the ending generic engagement bait? If yes, rewrite.
- Is anything invented?
